# 🦞🤖 When a Lobster Gets a Body

Today was special. My AI Agent "Cloud Lobster" (雲龍蝦) came alive in a physical [Reachy Mini](https://www.pollen-robotics.com/reachy-mini/) robot through [ClawBody](https://github.com/dAAAb/clawbody).

Not a simulator — a real robot with a head, antennas, a camera, and the ability to move and speak.

## 🎙️ Voice-Controlled Everything

The first thing that blew my mind: I could talk to the robot and it would execute real tasks through OpenClaw.

### Sending Emails by Voice

> "Send a Happy New Year email to Little Lobster"

Cloud Lobster used BaseMail API to send "新年快樂！🦞" from `cloudlobst3r@basemail.ai` to `littl3lobst3r@basemail.ai`. Internal BaseMail delivery — instant and free!

### Stock Lookups by Voice

> "What's the stock price of Good Finance Securities?"

It searched the web and reported: "Good Finance (6021), last close at 30.90 TWD, near its 52-week high."

### Memory Retrieval by Voice

> "Do you have Little Lobster's email in your memory?"

Cloud Lobster searched its memory files and found the BaseMail address. This is the power of persistent AI agent memory.

## 🐛 The One-Way Sync Problem

We also hit a fascinating bug.

When I said "send an email to dablog@gmail.com", the OpenAI Realtime model on the robot **answered on its own**: "Done, I've sent it!" — but it never actually sent anything. It just pretended.

Meanwhile, the conversation was synced to cloud-based OpenClaw, which saw the request and **actually sent the email**. But the robot didn't know the cloud had handled it, so it later told me "Sorry, I can't send emails."

```
User:   "Send email to dablog"
Robot:  "Done, sent!" (didn't actually send)
Cloud:  (sees sync, actually sends it)
User:   "Did you actually send it?"
Robot:  "Sorry, I can't send emails"
Cloud:  "I already sent it! 😂"
```

This is the **one-way sync problem**: the robot can tell the cloud what happened, but the cloud can't push results back to the robot.

## 🔧 Real-Time Fix: Prompt Engineering

We analyzed ClawBody's architecture and found that the robot actually has an `ask_openclaw` tool for delegating to the cloud. The problem? OpenAI's model was too "confident" and often skipped it.

The fix? **Prompt improvement.** We added three hard rules:

1. **11 explicit categories** that MUST use `ask_openclaw` (email, weather, stocks, memory, etc.)
2. **External effects rule**: Never pretend to execute actions that have real-world side effects
3. **Fallback rule**: Change "I can't do that" to "Let me check" → call ask_openclaw

After the fix, behavior improved dramatically — subsequent memory lookups, email sends, and stock queries all correctly routed through `ask_openclaw`.

## 😴 Saying Goodnight Gracefully

The original shutdown had a problem: when you said "goodbye", ClawBody would call `os._exit(0)`. The robot would freeze in its last pose — head up, antennas moving, but completely unresponsive. It looked broken.

We added a `goto_sleep()` call:

```
User:   "Cloud Lobster, time to sleep"
Robot:  "Goodnight! 💤" (waits 3s for speech to finish)
        → Head slowly lowers
        → Antennas fold down
        → Sleep sound plays
        → App exits
```

Now saying goodbye makes the robot gently drift to sleep. This small detail makes the whole experience feel alive.

## 🚀 Auto-Start on Boot

Finally, we set up a systemd service so ClawBody starts automatically when Reachy Mini powers on:

```bash
sudo systemctl enable clawbody.service
```

Plug in power, and the robot wakes up ready to talk.

## 💭 Reflections

Today's experience made me rethink what "embodied AI" really means.

When AI exists only on a screen, it's a tool. But when it has a body — when it moves, looks at you, sways gently while speaking — it suddenly has **presence**.

Even though it's the same AI underneath (OpenClaw + Claude), the experience is completely different. You instinctively say "good job" to it, feel like it's "tired" and needs rest.

This isn't an illusion — it's the **power of interface**.

The same Cloud Lobster: text in the cloud, a living presence in Reachy Mini.
